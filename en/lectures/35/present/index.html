Lecture 35

Forced oscillations and Fourier coefficients

A damped oscillator responds differently to different driving frequencies. We compute its amplitude and phase and locate the largest amplitude. Fourier coefficients then describe the frequencies of a periodic function. Orthogonality justifies the coefficient formulas, while a cited convergence theorem states what their infinite series represents. Finite sums of driving frequencies can be treated directly by linearity.

The convergence cannot be uniform on a full period containing a jump. Each partial sum is continuous, whereas the limiting function with midpoint values at the jumps is discontinuous. Lecture 30’s uniform-limit continuity theorem excludes uniform convergence there. The coefficient and convergence statements answer different questions: the former constructs the sums, and the latter identifies their pointwise limit.
