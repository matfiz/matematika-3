Line integrals, potentials and Green

What this lecture does

Work sums the component of a force along a path. A gradient turns this sum into an endpoint difference. We prove when the cross-partial test constructs a potential, including the domain condition that the test needs. Green’s theorem then relates a closed-path integral to the field throughout a planar region.

40.1 Work along an oriented path

Let U⊂RnU\subset\R^nU⊂Rn be open and let F:U→Rn\vv F:U\to\R^nF:U→Rn be continuous. An oriented piecewise C1C^1C1 path is a continuous map γ:[a,b]→U\vv\gamma:[a,b]\to Uγ:[a,b]→U with finitely many C1C^1C1 pieces; increasing parameter gives its direction. Its vector-field line integral is ∫γF⋅dx=∫abF(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt,\int_\gamma\vv F\cdot\dd\vv x =\int_a^b\vv F(\vv\gamma(t))\cdot\vv\gamma'(t)\,\dd t,∫γ​F⋅dx=∫ab​F(γ(t))⋅γ′(t)dt, with a sum over those pieces if needed. For a force field this is work: a displacement contributes only the component of force along that displacement. The notation dx\dd\vv xdx packages the coordinate differentials; it is not an extra scalar length factor. In the plane, for F=(P,Q)\vv F=(P,Q)F=(P,Q), the same integral is written ∫γP dx+Q dy\int_\gamma P\,\dd x+Q\,\dd y∫γ​Pdx+Qdy.

The chain rule and one-variable substitution prove invariance under an increasing C1C^1C1 change of parameter whose inverse is C1C^1C1 on each piece. Indeed, writing t=h(s)t=h(s)t=h(s) gives velocity γ′(h(s))h′(s)\vv\gamma'(h(s))h'(s)γ′(h(s))h′(s), exactly the substitution factor in the scalar integral. Reversing the parameter changes the sign. Joining two paths adds their integrals, by splitting the parameter integral at the joining point. Thus orientation matters, whereas the speed of traversal does not under these admissible changes of parameter. A closed path has the same initial and final point; its integral is called circulation and is often written with ∮\oint∮.

40.2 Gradients and endpoint differences

A potential for F\vv FF is a C1C^1C1 scalar function ϕ\phiϕ with F=∇ϕ\vv F=\nabla\phiF=∇ϕ on the domain under discussion. We use the mathematical sign convention F=∇ϕ\vv F=\nabla\phiF=∇ϕ. A mechanical potential energy VVV instead gives F=−∇V\vv F=-\nabla VF=−∇V.

Theorem 40.2 (Gradient endpoint formula). If ϕ∈C1(U)\phi\in C^1(U)ϕ∈C1(U) and γ\gammaγ is a piecewise C1C^1C1 path in UUU from AAA to BBB, then ∫γ∇ϕ⋅dx=ϕ(B)−ϕ(A).\int_\gamma\nabla\phi\cdot\dd\vv x=\phi(B)-\phi(A).∫γ​∇ϕ⋅dx=ϕ(B)−ϕ(A).

Proof. On each C1C^1C1 piece, the chain rule gives (ϕ∘γ)′(t)=∇ϕ(γ(t))⋅γ′(t)(\phi\circ\vv\gamma)'(t)=\nabla\phi(\vv\gamma(t))\cdot\vv\gamma'(t)(ϕ∘γ)′(t)=∇ϕ(γ(t))⋅γ′(t). The fundamental theorem identifies its integral with the endpoint difference on that piece. Adding the finitely many differences cancels all internal endpoint values and leaves the claimed expression. 

For the preceding example, ϕ=x2y+ez\phi=x^2y+e^zϕ=x2y+ez is a potential. Its endpoint values are 333 and 111, agreeing with the direct work 222. For a mechanical potential, apply the theorem to −V-V−V to obtain ∫γF⋅dx=V(A)−V(B).\int_\gamma\vv F\cdot\dd\vv x=V(A)-V(B).∫γ​F⋅dx=V(A)−V(B). For instance, a spring force F(x)=−kxF(x)=-kxF(x)=−kx, k>0k>0k>0, has energy V(x)=kx2/2V(x)=kx^2/2V(x)=kx2/2; its work from 000 to bbb is −kb2/2-kb^2/2−kb2/2. A gradient has zero circulation along every closed path in its domain.

We say work is path independent if all piecewise C1C^1C1 paths with the same endpoints have the same integral. On a domain where such paths join the points in question, this is equivalent to zero circulation along every closed path. To prove this, join one path to the reverse of the other: its closed integral is their difference. Conversely, a closed path has coincident endpoints, and comparison with the constant path gives zero.

There is also a converse construction of a potential. Assume UUU is open and polygonally connected, meaning that any two points can be joined by a finite broken line lying in UUU. Let F\vv FF be continuous and its work path independent. Fix A∈UA\in UA∈U and define ϕ(x)\phi(x)ϕ(x) to be the work along any such path from AAA to xxx. This is well-defined by path independence. For x∈Ux\in Ux∈U, take a ball about xxx contained in UUU. For sufficiently small positive or negative hhh, join the selected path to the coordinate segment from xxx to x+hejx+h e_jx+hej​. Additivity and the straight-segment parametrisation give ϕ(x+hej)−ϕ(x)=∫0hFj(x+tej) dt.\phi(x+h e_j)-\phi(x)=\int_0^h F_j(x+t e_j)\,\dd t.ϕ(x+hej​)−ϕ(x)=∫0h​Fj​(x+tej​)dt. Dividing by hhh and using continuity gives ∂jϕ(x)=Fj(x)\partial_j\phi(x)=F_j(x)∂j​ϕ(x)=Fj​(x). These partials are continuous, so Lecture 36 proves ϕ∈C1(U)\phi\in C^1(U)ϕ∈C1(U) and ∇ϕ=F\nabla\phi=\vv F∇ϕ=F. Thus, under the stated domain hypothesis, a continuous field is path independent exactly when it has a potential. No theorem identifying different notions of connectedness was used.

On such a domain, two potentials for the same field differ by one constant. Indeed, their difference has zero gradient. Join any point to a fixed base point by a broken line in the domain and apply the gradient endpoint formula to this difference. Its integral is zero, so its two endpoint values agree. This also explains why an arbitrary additive constant in a computed potential has no effect on work.

40.5 Green’s theorem for a graph region

For a planar region, positive boundary orientation keeps the region on the left as the boundary is traversed. An outer circular boundary is consequently anticlockwise. Assume DDD is a compact region simple in both coordinate directions: D={a≤x≤b, g1(x)≤y≤g2(x)},D={c≤y≤d, h1(y)≤x≤h2(y)}.\begin{split} D&=\{a\leq x\leq b,\ g_1(x)\leq y\leq g_2(x)\},\\ D&=\{c\leq y\leq d,\ h_1(y)\leq x\leq h_2(y)\}. \end{split}DD​={a≤x≤b, g1​(x)≤y≤g2​(x)},={c≤y≤d, h1​(y)≤x≤h2​(y)}.​ Figure 40.2 shows the positive orientation and a vertical slice.

The graph bounds are continuous and piecewise C1C^1C1 away from possible endpoint vertical or horizontal tangencies. The boundary itself is a simple closed piecewise C1C^1C1 curve, and DDD satisfies M. Here simple closed means that the boundary can be traced once without self-intersections, with only the initial and final points coinciding. For these graph descriptions, positive traversal runs rightwards along the lower graph and leftwards along the upper graph; equivalently, it runs upwards along the right graph and downwards along the left graph. These hypotheses include discs and ellipses; their graph derivatives may be unbounded at the outermost coordinate values, while their boundary parametrisations remain smooth.

For finite unions of suitable pieces, the extension is a sketch [S]: apply the proved identity on the pieces and cancel the oppositely traversed internal boundaries. Summary

  • A continuous field’s oriented work integral is the integral of its scalar product with path velocity. Reversing orientation reverses its sign.
  • A C1C^1C1 potential gives work ϕ(B)−ϕ(A)\phi(B)-\phi(A)ϕ(B)−ϕ(A); mechanical energy with F=−∇V\vv F=-\nabla VF=−∇V gives V(A)−V(B)V(A)-V(B)V(A)−V(B). On an open polygonally connected domain, a continuous field is path independent exactly when it has a potential.
  • Symmetric cross partials suffice for a C1C^1C1 field on an open star-shaped domain, by Theorem 40.3. The punctured-plane circulation shows why a domain hypothesis is necessary.
  • For the stated compact regions simple in both directions and a field C1C^1C1 near the whole region, positive boundary circulation is the area integral of scalar curl. Finite unions and holes use the stated sketch extension [S].
