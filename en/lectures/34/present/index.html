Lecture 34

Second-order linear equations

Characteristic roots provide solutions of a constant-coefficient equation. Factorisation proves that these solutions give every solution, including the repeated-root case. Initial position and velocity determine their coefficients. The same argument treats damping and exponential forcing, and first-order uniqueness identifies the exponential series for every complex argument.

Uses.

  • Summary 8: Product and chain rules and the elementary derivative table hold on their stated domains.

  • Summary 9: A real derivative which is identically zero on an interval gives a constant function.

  • Summary 16: Complex arithmetic, the exponential product law, and (eλt)′=λeλt(e^{\lambda t})'=\lambda e^{\lambda t}(eλt)′=λeλt apply with real time and complex constants.

  • Summary 21: A 2×22\times 22×2 linear system has a unique solution when its determinant is nonzero.

  • For exponential forcing, Equation (34.2) gives particular solutions with an additional factor ttt at a simple characteristic root and t2t^2t2 at a double root. Add the homogeneous solution to match initial data.
  • Equation (34.3) holds absolutely for every complex zzz. Its proof differentiates real component series and applies the proved complex first-order uniqueness theorem.
