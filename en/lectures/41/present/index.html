Lecture 41

Flux, surfaces and vector derivatives

Green’s theorem computes area and planar outward flux from a boundary. For a surface in space, flux needs both an area factor and a choice of normal direction. We define these quantities through parametrisations, check how coordinate changes affect them, and compute a sphere and a graph. The Cartesian differential operators prepare the boundary laws of Lecture 42.

41.4 Orientation and vector flux

An orientation chooses a continuously varying side of each regular surface piece, with consistent choices where pieces meet. In a parametrisation one choice of unit normal is n+=ru×rv∥ru×rv∥;n−=−n+.\vv n_+=\frac{\vv r_u\times\vv r_v}{\norm{\vv r_u\times\vv r_v}}; \qquad \vv n_-=-\vv n_+.n+​=∥ru​×rv​∥ru​×rv​​;n−​=−n+​. For a surface that bounds a solid, the outward normal points away from that solid. An open surface, such as a disc, needs an explicitly specified orientation, for example upward.

For a continuous vector field defined near an oriented surface, its flux is ∬SF⋅n dS\iint_S\vv F\cdot\vv n\,\dd S∬S​F⋅ndS. Reversing orientation reverses flux but leaves scalar area unchanged. Equation (41.2) shows why exchanging the parameter order reverses the induced normal. Summary

  • Green gives area ∮x dy=−∮y dx\oint x\,\dd y=-\oint y\,\dd x∮xdy=−∮ydx for positive boundary orientation under its stated hypotheses. Hence anticlockwise phase-plane traversal has ∮p dq=−area⁡\oint p\,\dd q=-\operatorname{area}∮pdq=−area.
  • Planar outward flux is ∮P dy−Q dx\oint P\,\dd y-Q\,\dd x∮Pdy−Qdx and equals the divergence area integral on Green’s domains, with the same proof status.
  • A regular surface patch has scalar area factor ∥ru×rv∥\norm{\vv r_u\times\vv r_v}∥ru​×rv​∥. Oriented flux uses the cross product itself, with its sign chosen to match the specified normal. Admissible reparametrisations preserve the resulting geometric integrals.
