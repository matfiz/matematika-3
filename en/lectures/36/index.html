Lecture 36

Differentiability in several variables

What this lecture does

A function of several variables may change in many directions. Partial derivatives record coordinate changes, but differentiability requires one linear approximation valid for all small increments. We prove a continuity criterion for that approximation and the matrix chain rule. For a scalar function its derivative is represented by the gradient, which determines directional changes and normals to level curves.

Summary

  • Differentiability means the single linear expansion Equation (36.1) with error o(∥h∥)o(\norm h)o(∥h∥) along every small increment. The derivative is unique, implies continuity and has Jacobian matrix JF(a)J_F(a)JF​(a).
  • Partial derivatives, even with continuity of the function, do not suffice. Theorem 36.3 supplies a sufficient condition: partial derivatives exist near the point and are continuous at the point.
  • Theorem 36.4 gives the Jacobian of a composition as the outer Jacobian evaluated at the inner image times the inner Jacobian.
  • For a differentiable scalar function, Duf=∇f⋅uD_uf=\nabla f\cdot uDu​f=∇f⋅u for unit uuu. The maximum is ∥∇f∥\norm{\nabla f}∥∇f∥; a zero gradient makes all these rates zero. A nonzero gradient is perpendicular to any given regular curve in its level set.
