Lecture 32

Motion along a path

A path specifies position as a function of time. Its first two derivatives give velocity and acceleration, while prescribed acceleration and initial data reconstruct the motion. We then resolve those derivatives in a rotating polar basis and along the tangent and normal to the path.

  • Component limits define vector derivatives. Continuous prescribed acceleration and initial position/velocity determine one C2C^2C2 motion by Equation (32.1).
  • Equations (32.2) and (32.3): a positive C2C^2C2 radius and a continuous C2C^2C2 angle give the polar velocity and acceleration, including both basis derivatives.

  • Theorem 32.3: for a regular C2C^2C2 path, curvature is ∥v×a∥/v3\norm{\vv v\times\vv a}/v^3∥v×a∥/v3. Where κ>0\kappa>0κ>0, acceleration is v˙T+κv2N\dot v\vv T+\kappa v^2\vv Nv˙T+κv2N; where κ=0\kappa=0κ=0, it is v˙T\dot v\vv Tv˙T.
