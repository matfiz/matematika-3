Lecture 38

Double integrals and the Gaussian

What this lecture does

A double integral sums a quantity spread over a planar region. We state the compact-domain integration rules used in this course, then use region geometry and Jacobians to evaluate those integrals. The Gaussian value follows by comparing compact discs and squares before taking ordinary improper limits. The same integral notation gives mass and the centroid of a plate with varying density.

38.1 Small-area sums

A circle qualifies even where its upper and lower graph derivatives become unbounded. The regions are closed and bounded; these are the compact regions meant in the integration statements here.

Standing fact M.

We use the following proper multiple-integral rules in two and three dimensions. The parameter-differentiation clause also covers proper integrals over a compact interval. The integrand is continuous on a compact region between graphs, or a finite union of such regions, with piecewise C1C^1C1 boundary. The corresponding compact graph regions and boundary conditions for three-dimensional solids are stated in Lecture 39 before their use.

  • Such multiple integrals exist. They equal the iterated integrals in any order for which the region is described by admissible graph pieces. This is the compact-region Fubini rule.

  • Let a C1C^1C1 map Φ\PhiΦ on a neighbourhood of a compact parameter region EEE map EEE onto DDD. If it is injective on the interior and has det⁡JΦ≠0\det J_\Phi\ne0detJΦ​=0 there, change of variables multiplies the integrand by ∣det⁡JΦ∣|\det J_\Phi|∣detJΦ​∣. Failures of injectivity or nonsingularity on the boundary, such as a polar origin or seam, are allowed.

  • For a fixed compact integration region, differentiating a proper integral with respect to a parameter is allowed when the integrand is C1C^1C1 jointly on a neighbourhood of the compact parameter–integration product. The derivative is the integral of the corresponding parameter derivative, at interior parameter values.

These are standing facts, rather than theorems proved in this course. Their sources are Zorich, Volume II, Chapter 11 and §17.1, and Stewart, §15.9. Results whose only unproved input is M count as proved under the course’s standing-fact policy. M makes no assertion about an improper multiple integral. Every unbounded-region limit below is justified separately.

Summary

  • Standing fact M supplies existence, Fubini, admissible coordinate changes and differentiation under proper compact integrals under its stated continuity and regularity hypotheses. It does not supply an improper multiple-integral rule.
  • A change of order must describe the same region. An admissible coordinate change multiplies the integrand by ∣det⁡J∣|\det J|∣detJ∣; polar area has factor rrr.
  • Compact discs and squares give the squeeze leading to Equation (38.3). The error function consequently has limits ±1\pm1±1.
  • Planar mass and first coordinate moments are double integrals. Their ratios give the centroid when the total mass is positive.
