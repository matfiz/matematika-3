Lecture 30

Uniform convergence and power series

For functions, convergence at each point can leave a large error elsewhere in the domain. Uniform convergence controls that error with one index. It preserves continuity and permits finite-interval integration of a series. A power series has this control on each closed interval strictly inside its radius.

Uses.

  • Summary 2: Every nonempty real set bounded above has a least upper bound.

  • Summary 3: Sequence limits and geometric limits obey arithmetic laws.

  • Summary 4: Bounded monotone sequences converge, squeeze holds, and n1/n→1n^{1/n}\to 1n1/n→1.

  • Summary 5: Continuity is defined relative to a function’s domain; elementary continuous functions remain continuous under finite sums.

  • Summary 8: The elementary derivatives and chain rule hold.

  • Summary 25: Finite integrals are linear and order preserving, with ∣∫f∣≤∫∣f∣|\int f|\leq\int|f|∣∫f∣≤∫∣f∣.

  • Summary 26: Continuous functions on closed finite intervals are Riemann integrable. The fundamental theorem differentiates their accumulation integrals.

  • Summary 29: Partial sums define convergence. Absolute convergence, geometric comparison, ratio/root tests and the alternating remainder bound hold.

Summary

  • Uniform convergence uses one index for all domain points. Pointwise convergence alone need not preserve continuity, as xnx^nxn on [0,1][0,1][0,1] shows.
  • Theorem 30.2: a convergent numerical majorant bounds every tail uniformly and gives pointwise absolute convergence.
  • Theorems 30.3 and 30.4: uniform limits preserve continuity and permit integration of continuous functions on a closed finite interval. Differentiation needs a separate argument.
  • Theorems 30.5 and 30.6: the radius gives absolute convergence inside and divergence outside; convergence is uniform on each closed interval strictly inside. Finite nonzero endpoints require separate tests.
  • Theorem 30.7: integrating coefficients gives a primitive inside the radius and preserves that radius. Its derivative follows from FTC.
  • Coefficient ratio or root limits, when they exist under the stated hypotheses, give R=1/LR=1/LR=1/L for the ratio limit and R=1/KR=1/KR=1/K for the root limit. The general limit-superior formula is cited [C].

Sheet 30 develops further uniform tests, the Dirichlet argument and the arctangent expansion by termwise integration.
