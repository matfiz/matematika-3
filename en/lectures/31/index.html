Lecture 31

Taylor series and termwise differentiation

What this lecture does

Uniform convergence alone does not preserve derivatives. For power series, the derivative series has the same radius, which provides the additional control. Taylor remainders then decide whether a function equals its series. We prove the elementary expansions and identify the complex exponential series on the imaginary axis.

31.2 Taylor coefficients and the remainder condition

Let FFF be a power-series sum with radius R>0R>0R>0. Repeated application of Theorem 31.1 preserves the radius at every finite derivative order. Summary

  • Theorem 31.1: the formal derivative has the same radius; inside it, termwise differentiation is valid. For R>0R>0R>0, repeated differentiation gives an=F(n)(0)/n!a_n=F^{(n)}(0)/n!an​=F(n)(0)/n! and unique coefficients.
  • A smooth function need not equal its Taylor series. Equality at a point is equivalent to the finite Taylor remainder tending to zero there.
  • Theorem 31.4: exponential, sine and cosine equal their absolutely convergent Taylor series for every real argument, by the factorial remainder bound.
  • Equation (31.4): the logarithmic series holds for −1<x≤1-1<x\leq1−1<x≤1. The value at 1 is proved by a finite Taylor remainder; the series diverges at x=−1x=-1x=−1.
  • Absolute complex convergence permits component and even/odd separation. The exponential factorial series equals eiθe^{\ii\theta}eiθ for every real θ\thetaθ.
