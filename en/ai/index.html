Learning with AI, not from it

An AI assistant will explain anything at any hour — and it will just as happily do your thinking for you, which is how people fail exams. Use it the first way: pick a section below and get a prompt that makes it explain, then hand the work back to you.

What a good session looks like

Lecture 3: choosing a threshold in an epsilon–N proof.

You

How do I choose N for 1/n → 0?

AI

Start with ε > 0. What inequality on n would make 1/n < ε? Try it, then check every n ≥ N.

Three habits that make the difference

  • Ask specifically. Name the definition, hypothesis or step you want to understand.
  • Do the work. Ask for a question and attempt it before requesting feedback.
  • Check every claim. Verify hypotheses, quantifiers and signs against the notes.

Working in pairs

  1. Pair up. Each of you authors a problem, and works your own model solution — privately.
  2. Swap. Hand over only the problem, not your solution.
  3. Solve. Each of you works the other's problem.
  4. Debrief without AI. Compare: did the solutions agree? Did the problem have a hole? Was the setter's own model answer actually right? This human step is the point of the exercise.

Two traps to avoid

The setter must verify their own model solution. Do not trust an AI-generated answer key. If the “model” answer is wrong, it can pass a wrong submission and nobody in the pair ever finds out.

The solver must resist pasting the problem in and asking for the answer. The skill being trained is reasoning and judgement, not retrieval. The whole exercise depends on not doing this.
